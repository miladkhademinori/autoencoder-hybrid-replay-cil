# Findings of the multi-agent audit (paper vs. implementation vs. results)

24 independent auditors produced 116 raw findings, merged into 41 clusters; the top 22 clusters were each checked by an adversarial verifier: 15 survived, 7 were refuted. The action plan is in plan.md and a completeness critique in critic.md. Everything below was generated by the audit agents and is quoted as is (tex:N = line N of the paper's LaTeX source).

## Verified findings

### K01: In the recon domain, old and new classes get separate latent-loss means, which weights each new class (l-1)x an old one

*Impact: high; verifier confidence 0.6; affects: ahr-cifar100, ahr-cifar10, ahr-svhn, mnist recon-domain ablation*

**Claim as verified:** In recon mode (SVHN, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 AHR; ahr/strategy.py:190-196 and :219-222), the latent loss is split into two separately averaged λ-weighted terms: decoded old exemplars and reconstructions of new samples. Each new sample therefore weighs b_mem/n times an old one: 3.9x at CIFAR-10 task 5 and 8.85x at CIFAR-100 task 10, and 1x at task 2. Eq. 1 (tex:191-196) together with tex:266 implies equal per-sample weights. This deviation is real and plausibly adds recency on the training replay among the latest old tasks: 42-54% of task-8/9 exemplars are predicted as task-10 classes, against 19% averaged over all old tasks. However, the test-time recency is much larger (53% of old test images go to the 10 newest classes). It is driven mainly by a gap between the replayed exemplars and real test images: test features lie 41 from their own CCE, replayed exemplars about 22. It is also driven by the growth of the CCE norm (14.8 at task 1 to 61.6 at task 10). The reweighting does not address either. On CIFAR-10, a single mean (AHR-lossless) shows even stronger recency. The fix should keep the total latent weight at 2λ, i.e. weights 2·b_mem/B and 2·n/B, so that it is not confounded with a halving of λ.

**Evidence:** PAPER: Eq. 1 (tex:193-194) is one sum over all samples with equal weight. tex:266: '1/l fraction ... new task, (l-1)/l fraction ... decoded ... in a statistically representative fashion'. tex:555 says an explicit bias correction 'proved unnecessary' because of this sampling; tex:450 gives AHR's BC as data equalization.
CODE: ahr/strategy.py:131-133 sets b_new=round(B/l). :193 takes l_lat = mean over z[n:] (decoded old exemplars only), weighted lam. :221-222 takes l_rn = mean over the n new reconstructions, weighted lam*lam_recon_new (=1, scripts/run.py:44-55). The input-domain branch :195 uses one mean; that branch serves MNIST and every AHR-lossless run.
LOGS: results/cifar100/ahr_s0.log end-of-task lat vs rn goes from 216/200 (task 3) to 384/103 (task 10); acc(memory) is 38-51%. Final per-task CIFAR-100 accuracy is [1.8,1.5,11.3,3.2,10.8,9.6,16.5,16.4,15.9,66.2]. On ahr_s0_ckpt_t10.pt, 54.7% of old-class test images are predicted as one of the 10 newest classes (uniform would be 10%). CIFAR-100 AHR-lossless, which uses one mean, shows no recency bias after task 8: [59.5,52.7,59.6,54.0,51.5,56.5,52.8,41.6].
MNIST controlled ablation adding the rn term raises newest-task accuracy (92->95-96) but lowers the final from 94.6 to 93.4 (REPRODUCTION.md ablation table).
Confounded with K02/K03, so the size is unmeasured. Estimate: several points, possibly more than 10, on CIFAR-100; 0-3 on CIFAR-10/SVHN, where l<=5.

**Proposed fix:** Use a single per-sample mean over all latent-loss inputs in recon mode and drop the separate lam*lam_recon_new*l_rn term; equivalently, weight l_lat by b_mem/(b_mem+n) and l_rn by n/(b_mem+n). Combine with K02 and rerun SVHN/CIFAR AHR.

**Expected effect:** These figures are my estimates, not measurements:
- CIFAR-100 AHR: final accuracy up by about 1-6 points (15.3 now). The gain would come mainly from tasks 7-9 and from the replay fit. It would close little of the 39-point gap, because most old-class errors come from the gap between memorised exemplars and real test images.
- CIFAR-10 / SVHN AHR: about -2 to +3 points, sign uncertain. The replay is already fitted to 93-97%, and cutting the new-task latent weight 4-5x can lower newest-task accuracy.
- MNIST: no effect on the reported configuration (input domain, one mean). The recon-domain ablation row (91.45) might move by about 1 point or less.

### K02: Recon-domain AHR never trains old classes on the test path; drift of stored codes turns 'fresh reconstruction' into a newest-task cue

*Impact: high; verifier confidence 0.8; affects: ahr-cifar100, ahr-cifar10 (small), ahr-svhn (unverified)*

**Claim as verified:** Confirmed from primary sources, with one correction to the mechanism. In recon-domain AHR (strategy.py:149,163,193 vs :172-179,219-222 vs test rule :81-92), old classes are trained only as decodes of stored frozen codes, and new classes only as round-trip reconstructions through the current autoencoder. The network learns to treat a fresh round trip through the current autoencoder as a newest-task cue.

On CIFAR-100 t10, old-class exemplars are 39% correct through the replay path. Their source images score 12% through the test rule, with 53% predicted as the newest task, and PSNR is equal. Old-class test images score 8%, with 57% predicted as the newest task.

The cue does not need code drift. A round trip of the replay image itself, phi(psi(phi(psi(m)))), also drops to 10.6%, and even task-9 exemplars drop from 28% to 10%. Drift of the stored spatial codes (0.44 for task 1, 0.16 for task 9) makes the gap on the source images grow with the age of the task, and substituting the stored spatial code recovers most of the replay-path accuracy (35.6%).

The gap remains under flip and reflect-crop augmentation. It is small on CIFAR-10 t5 (99 vs 93), where the old-class test deficit comes mostly from other causes.

The fix most likely to help is the one proposed: apply L_z to round-trip reconstructions of the decoded replays, so that every class is trained in the test domain. Re-encoding the stored codes or adding a code-consistency loss addresses only the drift part.

**Evidence:** PAPER: the test rule is argmin||phi(x)-p|| (tex:293). The recon domain is a reproduction deviation; REPRODUCTION.md §3.11 says it puts train and test inputs 'in the same domain for every class'.
CODE: old classes enter L_z only as C(phi(aug(psi_old(m)))) (strategy.py:149,163,193), with m frozen at storage (strategy.py:283-293, new_data=z[keep_new]). New classes enter only as C(phi(psi_cur(phi_cur(x)))) (strategy.py:172-179,219-222). At test time every class goes through phi(psi(phi(x))) (strategy.py:81-92).
PROBES (read-only, three independent auditors, no augmentation), results/cifar100/ahr_s0_ckpt_t10.pt: replay path 40.4-42.7% correct vs test path on the same source images 10.4-10.7%; newest-task share 15.8% -> 51-53%. PSNR is equal (22.7 vs 22.9 dB). ckpt_t3: 40.0 vs 33.7; ckpt_t6: 32.3 vs 16.0. Substituting the stored spatial part of the code recovers 38.8%; substituting the stored class part changes nothing. So the cue is spatial-code drift: |m_s-phi_s(x)|/|m_s| is 0.41 for task 1 and 0.16 for task 9. Task-1 exemplars: 52% via replay path vs 4% via test path.
CIFAR-10 split ckpt_t5: 99.4 vs 92.8, so the effect is small there.
This is separate from the border cue (K03). The fix is bounded above by the replay-path accuracy of about 40% (K06). Estimate: +10-15 points of old-class accuracy on CIFAR-100, about 0-5 on CIFAR-10.

**Proposed fix:** Compute x_rec = model(aug(x))[1] (eval mode, no_grad) for the whole minibatch, decoded replays included, and apply lam*||C(phi(x_rec))-p_y||^2 to every sample, averaged as one mean (K01). This also removes the border asymmetry (K03). Alternatives: re-encode stored codes each task (the literal Alg. 4), or add a code-consistency loss ||phi_s(psi_old(m))-m_s||^2.

**Expected effect:** These estimates are untested.

- CIFAR-100: old-class test accuracy at t10 is about 8-10% and old classes are 90% of the test set. Removing the cue could raise old-class accuracy by about +10-20 points, bounded by the replay-path fit of about 40%. That is about +8-15 points of final accuracy, from 15.3 to roughly 25-30. The paper reports 54.4, so most of that gap would remain.
- CIFAR-10: about +0-5 points. Exemplar sources already score 93% through the test path, so the old-class test deficit there comes mainly from generalisation and the border cue (K03).
- SVHN: unverified; no checkpoint was probed.

### K03: The zero-border task cue (§3.14) is stronger on CIFAR-100 and also present on SVHN; all running AHR jobs still use zero padding

*Impact: high; verifier confidence 0.75; affects: ahr-cifar100, ahr-cifar10, ahr-svhn*

**Claim as verified:** The zero-padding task cue of §3.14 also appears on SVHN and CIFAR-100 (verified on ckpt_t5 and ckpt_t10): a replay-style zero border sends almost no predictions to the newest task, and a new-sample-style border sends almost all of them there. Every reported AHR result and both local in-progress AHR runs (svhn split s0, cifar10 s1) use zero padding, and --pad-mode still defaults to 'constant' (run.py:155). However, reflect-padding runs for CIFAR-10 s0, CIFAR-100 s0 and SVHN split s0 were already launched in cloud sessions (results/cloud_jobs.txt; REPRODUCTION.md §3.14). So the fix is being tested, but not on the other seeds (svhn s1/s2, cifar10 s2 still zero-padded), and the SVHN/CIFAR-100 evidence is not yet in the write-up. Whether reflect padding recovers accuracy is unknown until those runs finish, because the replay path (decode then augment) and the new-sample path (augment then reconstruct) stay asymmetric.

**Evidence:** PAPER: silent on augmentation; tex:420 says decoded exemplars are 'nearly as effective as perfect exemplars'.
CODE: strategy.py:149-163 decodes replays and then augments the concatenated batch (data.py zero-pad crop). strategy.py:175 reconstructs new samples after augmentation, so their border is decoder-rendered. scripts/run.py:155 --pad-mode default 'constant'. The live processes (ps) are 'run.py --dataset cifar10 --method ahr --seed 1' and '--dataset svhn --method ahr ... --tag split', both without --pad-mode.
NEW EVIDENCE beyond §3.14:
- SVHN ckpt_t5, newest task: 89.5 as evaluated / 96.5 via new-sample path / 1.0 via replay path.
- CIFAR-100 ckpt_t10: adding a sharp border after decoding sends 1.7% of predictions to task 10 (56.8% clean) and raises accuracy from 13.7 to 19.0 without retraining; a decoder-rendered border sends 91.5% to task 10.
- CIFAR-10 ckpt_t5: the newest task's own training images score 74.3% clean vs 94.7% with a pre-AE zero crop. This matches the per-task AHR-minus-lossless profile [+0.2,-8.3,-21.0,-13.5,-22.6]: the loss is on the newest tasks (plasticity), not on retention.
Reflect padding removes only the border component; the path asymmetry in K02 remains.

**Proposed fix:** Make --pad-mode reflect the default for AHR. Preferably send every latent-loss input through the same augment -> current-AE path (the K02 fix covers both). Do not report the running zero-padded runs as the final configuration.

**Expected effect:** These are estimates, not measurements. Removing the cue should mainly help newest-task accuracy.
- CIFAR-10: most of the -22.6 newest-task gap to lossless, plus part of the -13 to -21 on tasks 3-4. Final accuracy might rise about 3-10 points (55.8 now, paper 77.1).
- CIFAR-100: the claim reports that simply adding the border at test time moves accuracy from 13.7 to 19.0. Expect a gain of about 3-10 points, far from closing the gap to 54.4; old-task accuracies of 1-16% point to a larger separate forgetting problem.
- SVHN: about 2-8 points (74.4 now, paper 93.0).
- MNIST: none, because augmentation is off there.
The cloud reflect runs will settle these numbers.

**Evidence:** BUDGET VERIFIED: tex:357 '#Total Exemplars 200 200 200 2000 2000'; tex:347 fixed memory; Table 3 tex:436-442 'BiC 20 (raw) ... 6M' (=2000x3072). ### K06: CIFAR-100 recon-domain classifier costs 14 points on task 1, before any replay, and never fits its own decoded replays

*Impact: high; verifier confidence 0.75; affects: ahr-cifar100*

**Claim as verified:** On CIFAR-100, the recon-domain AHR setting loses about 14 points on task 1 before any replay: 63.2 against 77.5 for the input-domain AHR-lossless run, whose training setup differs only in latent_domain and lam_recon_new. The deficit is in the encoder itself. With the same checkpoint, phi(x) on real test images gives 63.7 and phi(psi(phi(x))) gives 63.2, and it reaches only about 72-73% on its own task-1 training images. The cause is that the latent loss is applied only to the current model's roughly 22 dB reconstructions (strategy.py:190-193, 219-222); rn=97.9 against lat=54.0 for the input-domain run. Neither the test rule nor decoder memorisation explains it: memorisation touches only the decoder, and test and train PSNR are 22.17 and 22.50 dB. After task 2, the model classifies only 38-57% of its decoded replays correctly (38-51% from task 3 on), while memory PSNR stays at about 22.3 dB. The old-task collapse therefore comes from the classifier failing to fit its replays, not from the decoder drifting. REPRODUCTION.md §3.12 already states this qualitatively. The fix to try is putting the latent loss on real images (e.g. lat_real_first with the split latent), not switching only the test rule.

**Evidence:** PAPER: classifies phi(x) directly (tex:293). AHR 54.43 vs AHR-lossless 56.71 (tex:386,393) implies decoded replay costs about 2 points.
LOGS: results/scratch/c100/cifar100/ahr_s0_split_l1.log (the first two tasks of the reported run): 'after task 1: acc=63.20 ... acc(memory)=73.3', task 2 47.45 with acc(memory)=57.0. results/cifar100/ahr_lossless_s0.log: 'after task 1: acc=77.50'. Both use split latent, lam 1, jitter 0.25, seed 0.
CIFAR-10 and SVHN show no task-1 cost (96.75 vs 96.95; 98.4 vs 97.9). Memory PSNR stays at 22.2-22.5 dB and the mem term at <=0.6, so the decoder is not forgetting.
REPRODUCTION.md §3.12 calls the recon classifier 'weak' but gives no controlled pair.
Memorisation adds only +0.5 dB on CIFAR-100 (K22), so fidelity is a lever.

**Proposed fix:** Treat the CIFAR-100 gap as dominated by the recon-domain workaround rather than by forgetting. Prioritise making the input-domain rule work once K02/K03 are fixed, a mixed-domain loss, or higher-fidelity memory, over further RFA or lambda tuning.

**Expected effect:** This affects ahr-cifar100 only. Fixing the task-1 training signal could recover up to about 14 points of first-task accuracy (63 to about 77), and most of that would carry into early tasks. The final gap (15.3 vs 54.4) is about 39 points and is dominated by old-task collapse (final per-task accuracies of 1.8-16.5 on old tasks). That collapse follows from the classifier failing to fit its decoded replays, so repairing task 1 alone would likely add only a few points at the end unless the replays are also fitted. Switching only the test rule to phi(x) would change about 0.5 points.

### K08: The Adam / 50-epoch / batch-256 / no-weight-decay recipe leaves the CIFAR-100 softmax baselines under-converged

*Impact: medium; verifier confidence 0.6; affects: ft_e-cifar100, icarl-cifar100, joint-cifar100, joint-cifar10*

**Claim as verified:** Only CIFAR-100 Joint is under-trained. The repo trains it once, from scratch on all data, for 50 epochs (9,800 Adam steps, final train CE 0.98, 59.4%). The paper describes Joint as training "on all tasks seen so far" (tex:332, FACIL-style incremental Joint), which ahr/baselines.py:184-189 does not do. The paper does specify Adam, lr 1e-3, 50 epochs and batch 256 for all 19 strategies including Joint (tex:347, tex:504, Table 4), so the repo's recipe is faithful to the paper. Weight decay and the cosine schedule are the repo's own choices where the paper is silent. FT-E and iCaRL on CIFAR-100 are not under-converged on tasks 2-10 (FT-E CE 0.07-0.32, falling below 0.2 from task 3 on). Their 11-21 point gaps match the gaps on fully converged MNIST, SVHN and CIFAR-10 runs, so the optimiser recipe is not their main cause.

**Evidence:** PAPER: 'We adopt Adam' (tex:347). Table 4 is captioned 'for our AHR strategy' (tex:509), although tex:504 says it covers 19 strategies. Table 3 gives BiC/IL2M/EEIL 60 epochs (tex:436-442). Silent on weight decay and schedule.
CODE: scripts/run.py:36 (50 epochs, batch 256), run.py:83-84 (weight decay 0, cosine), ahr/common.py make_optimizer (Adam, coupled L2).
LOGS: results/cifar100/ft_e_s0.log runs 20 it/epoch on task 1 and 28 afterwards, ending task 1 at CE 0.272. **Proposed fix:** Add an --optimizer {adam,sgd} option with weight decay. Report the CIFAR-100 baselines and Joint under both the AHR recipe and a FACIL-like SGD recipe. State in REPRODUCTION.md that the paper gives no baseline hyperparameters.

**Expected effect:** joint-cifar100: an incremental Joint (as tex:332 describes) or a longer or SGD+weight-decay schedule plausibly adds about 5-10 points (59.4 to about 65-69). It is unlikely to reach 73.9, which is above typical ResNet-32 results on CIFAR-100. ft_e-cifar100 and icarl-cifar100: a better-fitted task 1 is worth about 0-3 points, direction uncertain; nothing supports +10, because the same-sized gap appears on converged benchmarks. joint-cifar10: weight decay might be worth about 1-3 points, unverified. MNIST and SVHN: no effect.

### K09: Joint is one from-scratch run, but the cited FACIL Joint is incremental warm-started training with 3-5.5x the steps

*Impact: medium; verifier confidence 0.7; affects: joint-cifar100, joint-cifar10*

**Claim as verified:** The paper defines Joint only as "trains on all tasks seen so far" (tex:332), cites FACIL, and reports accuracy after the last task (tex:341). - The task-1 CCEs of results/cifar10/ahr_s0_ckpt_t1.pt and results/svhn/ahr_s0_split_ckpt_t1.pt agree to max|diff| 0.0182 (I reproduced this), so the task-1 CCEs do not depend on the data. The no-jitter class means are 0.090 apart on CIFAR-100 (the log's |init spread|=0.090).
- No run of the final split-latent, lam=1 configuration without jitter exists.

Why the claim still fails as stated:
1. **The fix is already done.** The proposal says to document the jitter as a deviation from Alg. 2, but REPRODUCTION.md already does:
   - the §2 table row "RFA initial positions | class means (Alg. 2), plus isotropic Gaussian jitter of norm 0.25 d for CIFAR-10/100";
   - §3.12, which reports this exact 33.9 vs 12.6 pair and the reason for it.
2. **"Replaces" is overstated.** The noise swamps the class means only on task 1. There the encoder is still random and its class means are 0.09 apart. Alg. 2 without jitter would also make them 20 apart: the no-jitter run shifts them by a mean of 41.4 from the 0.09-spaced means. On later tasks the data still counts. The jittered init spread is 7-23 on CIFAR-100 and 7-14 on CIFAR-10, while jitter alone would give a spread of about 7.
3. **The -21 points are a CCE-radius effect, and lambda was retuned afterwards.** The §3.12 rows share one setting (spatial latent, lam 0.3) and show:
   - jitter lowers |p| from 42 to 14 and accuracy from 33.9 to 12.6;
   - a smaller spacing (|p| = 7) lowers it further, to 10.4;
   - raising lambda with jitter on gives 56.1 at lam 3 (ahr_s0_j25_lam3.log), well above the no-jitter 33.9.
   So the effect works through the size of the latent loss (roughly lam·|p|²), not through the CCEs being data-independent. The final configurations (split latent, lam 1: 63.2 on task 1) were tuned with jitter on. The pair therefore says nothing about the sign or size of the effect in the final configuration.
4. **No harm is visible where the task-1 CCEs are data-independent.** Every final 2-class run reaches the ceiling on task 1 with jitter on: CIFAR-10 s0 96.7, CIFAR-10 s1 96.2, SVHN split 98.4. With 2 CCEs, jitter on task 1 only rotates a pair of points that end up 20 apart with |p| of about 10.5. That rotation is irrelevant for an encoder trained from scratch. Proposing "jitter on task 1 only" also contradicts the claim's own argument that task-1 data-independence is the problem.
5. **"Several points in either direction" has no evidence behind it.** Seed-to-seed noise on CIFAR-10 is already about 3 points (after task 2: 77.0 for s0 vs 80.2 for s1).

The only defensible remainder is that the jitter was never ablated in the final configuration. That is a gap in the ablations, not a demonstrated cause of the gap to the paper.

### K14: On CIFAR-100, iCaRL is limited by plasticity (new-task accuracy 53-62%), consistent with KD weighted T^2=4 on an under-converged network

What checks out:
- The paper says nothing about iCaRL's loss, KD weight or temperature. Appendix B, Table tab:bench (tex ~506-539), lists only lr, momentum, epochs, batch size and exemplar counts. Line 320 says only that "iCaRL fuses exemplar replay and LwF" and "classifies via the closest centroid".
- The code matches the quote. ahr/baselines.py:86-88 computes `F.kl_div(log_softmax(out[:, :n_old]/T), softmax(out_old/T), batchmean) * T * T`, weighted by kd_lambda. scripts/run.py:169-170 sets the defaults kd_lambda=1.0 and T=2.0.
- The log numbers are right (results/cifar100/icarl_s0.json acc_matrix, ft_e_s0.json):
  - Diagonals for tasks 2-10 run from 52.8 to 62.5 for iCaRL and from 68.8 to 85.4 for FT-E.
  - The means 59.7 and 78.2 only come out if task 1 is included, which the quoted ranges leave out (minor inconsistency).
  - Mean forgetting is 24.1 for iCaRL and 56.8 for FT-E.
  - iCaRL's end-of-task CE is 0.35-0.61, against 0.07-0.32 for FT-E.

Why the conclusion ("limited by plasticity"; a weaker KD should raise the final 38.0) is not supported:
1. **The comparison with FT-E is confounded.** iCaRL is scored with NME, which has no recency bias (baselines.py:39-43; paper line 320 also has iCaRL classify by closest centroid). FT-E is scored with a softmax head that is heavily biased toward the newest task: after task 10 it scores 85.4 on the new task but 13-27 on each old task. Plain FT scores 77-88 on the new task, which is effectively within-task accuracy. So FT-E's 78 is inflated by that bias and is not a measure of plasticity.
2. **The right reference is Joint, and iCaRL is already close to it.** Joint's per-task accuracies are [60.8, 55.2, 63.0, 56.4, 57.8, 61.8, 60.7, 56.2, 64.9, 57.5], measured over all 100 classes. iCaRL's accuracy on tasks 2-10 right after learning each one averages 57.7, against 59.3 for Joint on the same classes, even though iCaRL is scored against fewer classes. The plasticity shortfall is about 1-3 points.
3. **Forgetting is what drives the final gap.** Final iCaRL accuracy is 38.0 = 0.1 x 54.7 (task 10) + 0.9 x 36.1 (tasks 1-9). Tasks 1-9 start at a mean of 60.2 and end at 36.1. Even if task 10 reached FT-E's biased 85.4, the final would rise by only about 3 points. Beyond task 2, the new task is usually the best-scoring one (for example after task 5: [50.4, 41.0, 51.3, 50.5, 61.7]), so new classes are not being held back.
4. **The KD is weaker than the reference form, not stronger.** The docstring (baselines.py:5) claims "iCaRL as in FACIL", but FACIL's iCaRL uses a sigmoid BCE summed over every old class with lamb=1. That gives a per-logit KD gradient on each of the 90 old logits, far stronger than a batch-mean softmax KL. Cutting the weight by 4x (kd_lambda 0.25) moves further away from the reference. It would most likely increase the forgetting that dominates the result, so the net effect is uncertain or negative.
5. **The KD form cannot explain the gap to the paper.** FT-E, which has no KD, is 21.4 points below the paper (27.1 vs 48.5), a larger gap than iCaRL's 11.4 (38.0 vs 49.4). A shared protocol difference is the main cause, not KD weighting.

The predicted gain from kd_lambda 0.25 cannot be tested here (no training allowed), and the primary logs contradict the reasoning behind it.

### K20: MNIST AHR-lossless ends 3 points below the paper because newest-task accuracy degrades, which points at the loss weights

The observations are accurate, but the cause the claim draws from them and the effect it predicts are both contradicted by results already in the repo.

What checks out:
- The paper leaves lambda unset. Eq. 1 (tex:193-198) says only "λ is a hyperparameter", and Table 4 in Appendix B (tex:506-535) lists no loss weights.
- Alg. 3 (tex:283-285) uses unit-weight, non-squared distillation norms. The code uses lam 0.3, alpha_z 0.01, alpha_x 1.0 and squared norms (scripts/run.py:42-43 and :115; ahr/strategy.py:180-188).
- results/mnist/ahr_lossless_s0.json has final 95.34 (3-seed mean 95.11) and diagonal 100/98.1/98.2/96.6/91.8. Seeds 1 and 2 reach 92.9 and 92.3 on task 5. Joint scores 98.18 on classes 8/9, iCaRL s0 95.5 and lossy AHR s0 92.1.

Why the conclusion fails:
1. A controlled run rules out the loss weights. ahr_lossless_mini_s0 differs from ahr_lossless_s0 only in n_exemplars (200 vs 7840); I diffed the stored args. It uses the same lam 0.3, alpha_x 1, alpha_z 0.01 and squared norms, yet reaches 98.6 on the newest task (seeds 1/2: 98.9/98.7, with diagonals of 98-99 throughout). The loss weights therefore do not cap plasticity at about 92. The drop with 7,840 exemplars comes from well-kept old classes (3/5, 4/7) competing with 8/9 at the nearest-CCE classifier. This is the normal stability-plasticity trade-off, not under-fitting of the new classes.
2. Raising newest-task accuracy did not raise final accuracy. The recon_new runs add more latent pull on new-task samples (lam_recon_new=1; otherwise identical args). Across 3 seeds they raise the task-5 diagonal from 92.3 to 96.4 on average (91.8→96.4, 92.9→96.8, 92.3→95.9). Final accuracy falls from 95.1 to 94.4 (95.3→94.5, 94.6→94.7, 95.4→94.1) because the old tasks lose more (s0: task 2 95.2→92.1, task 4 95.3→92.5).
3. The arithmetic caps the benefit. The newest task is 1/5 of the final average. Lifting it from 91.8 to Joint's 98.2 with the old tasks unchanged adds at most about 1.3 points, less than half of the 2.8-3.0 point gap. The old tasks 2-4, all around 95.2-95.3 in the final row, account for about the same share of the gap as the newest task.

The paper does leave the loss weights unspecified, so a re-tune on a validation split is legitimate. But no evidence shows that the loss weights cause the newest-task drop, or that re-tuning them would recover most of the gap.

### K21: The paper says AHR uses EEIL's balanced training, but AHR has no end-of-task balanced phase

Parts of the claim are true. tex:320 first defines EEIL's balanced training as "at the end of each training session an equal number of exemplars from all classes is used for a limited number of iterations" and then says "AHR employs balanced training (from EEIL)". The code also has no such phase for AHR: learn_task (ahr/strategy.py:363-374) calls placement, train_task, populate_memory and memorize, and memorize fits only the decoder. The EEIL phase exists only in baselines.py:146-175.

Calling this a missing part of the paper's method is not supported, though:
1) The paper's formal procedure has no end-of-task balanced step. Alg. 1 (tex:228-231) is CCE_Placement, then HAE_Train, then Memory_Population, then delete the old model. Alg. 3 (tex:278-286) trains on D for E epochs.
2) The paper spells out what AHR takes from Castro, and it is the minibatch composition, not a separate phase. tex:555: "Because AHR samples the tasks/classes for its minibatch in a statistically representative manner during training inspired by [Castro], incorporating an explicit bias-correction strategy such as LT proved unnecessary." tex:266 gives the recipe: a 1/l fraction from the new task and an (l-1)/l fraction decoded from memory. tex:450 calls Castro-style bias correction implicit "data equalization", and Table 2 gives AHR BC=I.
3) The Appendix B hyperparameter table (tex:505-535) lists no balanced-phase epochs or learning rate for AHR.

The code implements that reading: strategy.py:131-132 sets b_new = round(B/(t+1)) and b_mem = B - b_new, and strategy.py:148 samples uniformly from the class-balanced memory (memory.py:23-27). Every benchmark has the same number of classes per task, so each minibatch is already about evenly split across classes. For example, CIFAR-10 at task 5 gets 26 new samples for 2 classes and 102 exemplars for 8 classes, about 13 per class. CIFAR-100 at task 10 gets about 2.6 per class for both old and new classes.

The second half of the rationale is also already handled on CIFAR and SVHN: with latent_domain='recon' and lam_recon_new=1 (strategy.py:190-196 plus the recon-new term), old and new classes both reach the latent loss through the decoder.

Would it affect results? The +12.5 cited from K04 is real: MNIST FT-E ends at 71.0/71.7/73.8, and with bft30 at 84.2/85.1/84.9. But FT-E is a softmax classifier trained on the imbalanced union of new data and exemplars, so it does not carry over to AHR's nearest-CCE classifier. On MNIST, AHR shows no recency bias: the last row of acc_matrix is [98.3, 94.2, 95.5, 95.6, 92.1], with the newest task lowest. So "affects ahr-mnist" is refuted. Recency is visible on SVHN [77.8, 63.9, 67.4, 75.3, 87.8], CIFAR-10 [59.6, 33.6, 44.7, 68.7, 72.4] and above all CIFAR-100, where the newest task scores 66.2 and old tasks score 1.5-16.5. Even so, the paper's own iCaRL (49.4 on CIFAR-100) and AHR reach their numbers with no such phase, so this cannot explain the 15.3 vs 54.4 gap. What an end phase could add over the current balanced minibatches is limited to evening out diversity (new classes cut down to their exemplars) and a lower learning rate, and nothing has been run to measure that. It is an optional extra, not a documented deviation.
